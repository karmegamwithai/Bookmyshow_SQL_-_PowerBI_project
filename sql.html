Important data validation rules from the BRD

  • Check missing values and duplicate records across all nine tables.
  • Verify primary keys and foreign-key relationships; validate date/time types and rating ranges.
  • Exclude failed and pending transactions from recognized revenue.
  • Prevent duplicate revenue calculations when joining bookings, seats and payments.
  • Validate ticket quantities against seat records where seat-level data is complete.
  • Calculate occupancy at show level using booked seats and screen capacity; do not sum repeated screen capacity across shows.

Assumptions and data gaps to resolve

  • The BRD notes that payments currently has no payment amount column. Add amount DECIMAL(10,2) or identify a trusted actual-payment source.
  • Define seat statuses (booked, available, cancelled, refunded) and clarify whether shows.available_seats is a current snapshot or historical value.
  • Clarify whether seats.charges is an additional charge or the full seat price.
  • Agree on a successful-booking definition and reconcile it with payment status.
  • Use booking_date for booking trends and payment_date for payment revenue trends.

The supplied BRD specifies table purposes and relationships, not a complete physical schema. The downloadable SQL therefore uses conventional assumed column names and is intended to be adapted to your database.
